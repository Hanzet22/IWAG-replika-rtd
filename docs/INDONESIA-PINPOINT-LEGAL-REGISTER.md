## 2. Reported PP 33/2026 — primary-source verification unresolved

Earlier research surfaced secondary-source reports of **Peraturan Pemerintah Nomor 33 Tahun 2026 tentang Peraturan Pelaksanaan Undang-Undang Nomor 27 Tahun 2022 tentang Pelindungan Data Pribadi**. Those sources report promulgation on 16 July 2026, a six-month commencement period under Pasal 225, and a future commencement date of 16 January 2027.

**That report is not verified against an official central promulgation record.** The BPK and Setneg official searches conducted for this review did not return a verified central record or an official final Gazette PDF. Search results surfaced legal-information sites and a PDF mirrored on a local-government host. Those are not sufficient to certify the exact Gazette/TLN identifiers or article-level wording.

Accordingly, as at **25 November 2026**:
- do not treat PP 33/2026 as an in-force regulation;
- do not cite **LN 2026 No. 88**, **TLN No. 7190**, **Pasal 225**, or any other PP 33/2026 locator as verified law in a public V2 policy;
- do not rely on secondary summaries of its DPIA, DPO, transfer, breach or sanction details as binding provisions;
- retain the report only as an unverified future-instrument watch item until the official Gazette/central JDIH text is located.

The already verified **UU 27/2022** and other currently in-force instruments remain the legal baseline.

## 3. Child online safety — PP 17/2025 scope and source status of Permenkomdigi 9/2026

### PP 17/2025 — verified in force

**Peraturan Pemerintah Republik Indonesia Nomor 17 Tahun 2025 tentang Tata Kelola Penyelenggaraan Sistem Elektronik dalam Pelindungan Anak** is in force from **27 March 2025**.

- Type/number/year: Peraturan Pemerintah No. 17 Tahun 2025.
- Gazette: LN 2025 No. 36; TLN No. 7105.
- Official BPK record: https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/316698/pp-no-17-tahun-2025
- Official text: https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/unduh/id/965/t/peraturan+pemerintah+nomor+17+tahun+2025

Pinpoint provisions relevant to a website:

- **Pasal 1 angka 1–7:** definitions of Anak, Sistem Elektronik, PSE, Produk/Layanan/Fitur, Data Pribadi and child-data impact assessment.
- **Pasal 2 ayat (1)–(4):** child-protection duties for electronic systems and minimum-age information, child-user verification and reporting mechanism within the stated scope.
- **Pasal 3 ayat (3):** private PSE scope includes products, services and features specifically designed for, or that may be used or accessed by, children.
- **Pasal 4 ayat (1) huruf a–b and ayat (2) huruf a–e:** indicators include published/internal terms and policies, strong evidence of routine child users, advertising directed to children, child-attractive design and substantial similarity to services proven to be used by children.
- **Pasal 4 ayat (3):** further indicators are to be determined by Ministerial Decision.
- **Pasal 5 ayat (1)–(9):** risk classification and self-assessment. Pasal 5 ayat (3) huruf a–g lists contact with strangers, harmful content, child-consumer exploitation, child-data security, addiction, psychological-health impact and physiological-health impact.
- **Pasal 7 ayat (1) huruf a–j, ayat (2) huruf a–c and ayat (3):** child-protection duties and prohibitions, including parental/guardian authorisation, child-data impact assessment, high-privacy defaults, clear information, age-appropriate functions, partner safeguards and restrictions on covert practices, precise child geolocation and child profiling.
- **Pasal 8:** best interests of the child and priority for child rights/protection over commercial interests.
- **Pasal 9 ayat (1)–(5):** parental/guardian authorisation before a child may use an in-scope product, service or feature, subject to the provision's specific arrangements.
- **Pasal 10–11:** high-privacy defaults and information for users.
- **Pasal 19:** restrictions on child profiling, with stated exceptions.
- **Pasal 20–22:** age limits, age ranges, account age rules and age-verification safeguards.
- **Pasal 23 ayat (1)–(2):** accessible reporting tools and operational follow-up for child-rights problems.
- **Pasal 49:** maximum two-year adjustment period from promulgation. With promulgation on 27 March 2025, the outer adjustment date is **27 March 2027**.

**Application to V2:** The absence of accounts, comments, chat or uploads lowers contact and UGC risks but does not automatically exclude the website. V2 must document whether the website or any feature is designed for, or may be accessed by, children under Pasal 3 ayat (3) and Pasal 4 ayat (1)–(2). Audience composition is not currently verified. See `docs/CHILD-ACCESS-PP17-SCOPE-ASSESSMENT.md`.

**Locator standard:** no statutory line numbers are published. Use Pasal/ayat/huruf/angka; do not invent “baris” numbers.

## 4. PP 71/2019 and UU 1/2024



### PP 71/2019

* **Pasal 1 angka 4–6** defines PSE and public/private-scope PSE broadly enough that a privately operated website can be an electronic system.
* **Pasal 3 ayat (1)** requires systems to be reliable, secure and responsibly operated; **Pasal 3 ayat (2)** concerns responsibility for proper system operation.
* **Pasal 6** addresses registration of private-scope PSE; whether a small informational site must register depends on the implementing registration categories and its activities, not simply the existence of a static webpage.
* **Pasal 14 ayat (1)–(5)** concerns lawful personal-data processing and protection. For post-2022 processing, it must be read together with the more specific UU 27/2022.
* **Pasal 15** requires governance procedures and audit trails; **Pasal 22–31** address security, continuity and electronic-system operation.
* **Pasal 46–51** govern electronic contracts and therefore matter when registration, ticketing, paid services or other contracting functions are added—not for ordinary reading of free information.
* **Pasal 95–96** concern unlawful electronic information and access termination, including blocking, account closure or content deletion.

### UU 1/2024

UU 1/2024 is an amending Act, so citations must identify its amendment numbers rather than treating every provision of the consolidated ITE Law as newly enacted in 2024.

* **Article I angka 3, new Pasal 13A**, concerns electronic-certification services including website authentication; it does not compel every static site to purchase such a certificate.
* **Article I, new Pasal 16A ayat (1)–(4)** requires PSEs to protect child users, prioritise children’s rights, provide age-verification mechanisms and provide accessible reporting mechanisms for children, parents or guardians. **Pasal 16B** provides implementing/enforcement authority.
* **Article I, new Pasal 18A**, governs specified international electronic contracts directed to Indonesian users; it becomes relevant if the site forms contracts, subscriptions or ticket sales with Indonesian users.
* Amended **Pasal 40** authorises government-ordered system adjustments and access termination. For UGC, moderation exposure increases where comments, uploads, forums or social features are provided.

## 5. Consumer protection and electronic commerce

### UU 8/1999

The key provisions are **Pasal 1 angka 2** (consumer) and **angka 3** (business actor); **Pasal 4 huruf a–i** (consumer rights); **Pasal 7 huruf a–g** (business-actor duties); **Pasal 8–17** (prohibited goods/services, misleading offers, promotions and advertising); **Pasal 18** (prohibited standard clauses); and **Pasal 19** (compensation/liability).

A free editorial or informational site that neither offers nor supplies goods/services to consumers is not ordinarily the consumer transaction at which these duties are directed. They become material if the operator sells tickets or merchandise, charges subscriptions, accepts bookings or promotes its own paid services. Merely linking to the event organiser’s independent ticket service does not normally transfer the organiser’s seller obligations to the site operator, provided the site does not act as seller, agent or joint contracting party and does not misrepresent that relationship.

### PP 80/2019

* **Pasal 2 huruf a–j** sets PMSE’s regulatory scope.
* **Pasal 11** requires PMSE business actors to satisfy general legal requirements.
* **Pasal 13 ayat (1) huruf a–c and ayat (2) huruf a–e** requires correct, clear and honest identity, goods/services and transaction information and compliance with advertising ethics.
* **Pasal 14** concerns suitable electronic systems for domestic/foreign PPMSEs.
* **Pasal 23 huruf a–b and Pasal 24 ayat (1)–(3)** concern unlawful content and secure, reliable, responsible systems.
* **Pasal 25 ayat (1) huruf a–b and ayat (2) huruf a–h** requires retention of transaction and related data.
* **Pasal 26 huruf a–b** requires consumer-rights protection and observance of fair competition; **Pasal 27 ayat (1) and ayat (2) huruf a–e** requires a consumer complaint service.
* **Pasal 28 ayat (1)–(4)** and **Pasal 31** concern valid transaction evidence; **Pasal 32 ayat (1)–(2)** concerns electronic advertising.
* **Pasal 58 ayat (1)–(2)** and **Pasal 59 ayat (1)–(4)** concern personal data in PMSE; these must now be read with UU 27/2022.
* **Pasal 60 ayat (1)–(2)** concerns electronic payment. **Pasal 80 ayat (1)–(5)** supplies administrative sanctions.

These provisions are activated by **PMSE activity**. They do not turn a read-only independent event-information site into the merchant or event organiser merely because it discusses an event.

### Permendag 19/2026

Permendag 19/2026 is also directed to the **conduct of PMSE business**, including PMSE actors and business models, business requirements, electronic advertising, domestic-product prioritisation, foreign-trade representative offices, AI use, supervision and sanctions. It does not ordinarily govern a non-commercial read-only site. It should be applied if the operator becomes a merchant or PPMSE—for example by operating a marketplace, ordering interface, checkout, paid booking or other system through which trade is conducted. The operative text and its actor-specific definitions should be checked before attributing a merchant, PPMSE or intermediary duty to a mere publisher.

## 6. Copyright

UU 28/2014 applies immediately to website content whether or not the site is commercial:

* **Pasal 4**: copyright comprises moral and economic rights.
* **Pasal 5 ayat (1) huruf a–e**: authorial attribution, name/pseudonym, alteration and integrity-related moral rights.
* **Pasal 8 and Pasal 9 ayat (1) huruf a–i, ayat (2)–(3)**: exclusive economic rights—including publication, reproduction, adaptation, distribution, performance, communication and rental—and the need for permission to exercise them commercially.
* **Pasal 40 ayat (1)** lists protected works relevant to websites, including writing, visual works, photographs, audiovisual works, compilations and computer programs.
* **Pasal 43–51**, especially **Pasal 44 ayat (1) huruf a–d**, contain specified limitations/exceptions; attribution alone is not a substitute for permission unless an exception actually applies.
* **Pasal 54–56** concern prevention and enforcement of copyright infringement through information-technology systems.

Therefore the independent site operator must clear rights for copied event logos, photographs, artwork, maps, text, video, music, fonts and code, while preserving attribution and integrity where moral rights apply. The organiser remains responsible for material it publishes on its own systems unless it licenses or supplies that material for the independent site.

## 7. Disability and accessibility

### UU 8/2016

Relevant rights include **Pasal 18 huruf a–b** (accessibility), **Pasal 19 huruf a–b** (public services) and **Pasal 24 huruf a–d** (expression, communication and information, including accessible information and communication media). Institutional duties concerning buildings and public services appear principally in **Pasal 97–104** and **Pasal 105–109**.

These provisions most clearly bind government, public-service providers, public facilities and parties delivering services to the public. They do not contain a simple provision stating that every private static website must satisfy a named web-accessibility technical standard. Nevertheless, making the site perceivable, keyboard-operable, understandable and compatible with assistive technology is the prudent way to avoid creating access barriers—especially where the website itself provides a public-facing service.

### PP 42/2020

PP 42/2020 implements UU 8/2016 **Pasal 104 ayat (4), Pasal 108 and Pasal 109 ayat (4)**. Its subject matter is accessible housing, public services and disaster protection. Its strongest application is therefore to:

* the **event organiser**, for event services, registration, communications and reasonable service accommodations;
* the **venue/facility operator**, for physical access, entrances, routes, toilets, seating, signage, emergency arrangements and other building/facility matters; and
* public-service bodies, for accessible service procedures and communications.

A separate read-only publisher is not automatically responsible for the venue’s physical accessibility. It must, however, avoid falsely describing access and should present any accessibility information it publishes in an accessible format.

## 8. Practical allocation by website feature

**Applies to the present read-only informational site:** PP 71’s baseline system-security duties; UU ITE rules on lawful content and governmental access orders; UU 28/2014 copyright; UU 27/2022 if server logs, analytics identifiers, cookies, emails or other personal data are processed; and accurate presentation of any consumer/event information.

**Triggered by child-access scope:** PP 17/2025 must be assessed even where there are no accounts; the key threshold is whether the website/product/service/feature is designed for or may be accessed by children under Pasal 3 ayat (3) and Pasal 4 ayat (1)–(2). If in scope, complete the risk assessment and address applicable safeguards, including Pasal 5, Pasal 7–11 and related provisions. Account age bands, account verification and any final Permenkomdigi 9/2026 procedures must be checked separately against the final gazetted text. The reported PP 33/2026 remains unverified and is not cited as current law.

**Triggered by transactions:** UU 8/1999, PP 80/2019, Permendag 19/2026, PP 71/2019 electronic-contract provisions and UU ITE Pasal 18/18A. Ticketing, payment, checkout, paid subscriptions, merchandise and booking functions are the main triggers.

**Triggered by UGC/social features:** additional content-reporting, moderation, child-interaction and access-termination exposure under UU ITE, PP 71/2019 and the child-safety regime. Comments, forums, chat, uploads and direct messaging should not be treated as equivalent to static publication.

**Event-organiser/venue obligations:** ticket contracts and refunds, attendee registration records, payment security, on-site child safeguarding, physical accessibility, accessible event services, emergency procedures and venue facilities remain with the organiser or venue unless the website operator actually undertakes those functions or jointly determines their operation.
