# IWAG V2 — Legal Drafting and Approval Gates

**Status:** Engineering requirements for nonbinding drafts only.  
**Review date:** 24 November 2026.  
**No document in this repository is an official IWAG policy or operative contract.**

## 1. Governing principles

- Determine the actual operator, controller, contracting party and policy owner before naming a responsible entity.
- Separate website rules from event admission rules, venue rules, staff procedures, ticketing conditions and third-party community rules.
- Determine jurisdiction from establishment, territorial processing, service targeting, monitoring, commercial activity and feature classification. Mere global website accessibility does not automatically make every country's law applicable.
- Use primary legal texts and current official registers. Record issuer, jurisdiction, instrument, status, effective date, applicable trigger, source URL and verification date.
- Do not invent an email address, complaint channel, refund policy, response SLA, moderator team, DPO, security control or retention period.
- Preserve mandatory statutory rights; avoid blanket waivers, absolute discretion, universal no-refund language or exclusion of liability that conflicts with applicable law.
- Draft in plain language and translate the complete document, not only its title and banner.
- Require operator approval and qualified legal review before a draft is dated as effective or presented as binding.

## 2. Canonical document boundaries

| Document | Covers | Must not claim |
|---|---|---|
| Terms and Conditions | Use of the independent website; real transactions only if later introduced; external-link boundaries; changes and disputes. | That the project sells passes, registers attendees, controls admission or binds IWAG. |
| Privacy Policy | Actual controller, data categories, purposes, lawful bases, recipients, processors, transfers, retention, security and rights. | That no data is processed at all; infrastructure providers may process connection metadata. |
| Code of Conduct | Behaviour in spaces actually controlled by the adopting operator; consent, harassment, discrimination, safety boundaries, reporting and procedural fairness. | Authority over the event, venue, external communities, staff or third-party servers. |
| Cookie Policy | Actual cookies and device storage, including provider-controlled technologies and user choices. | That local storage is an HTTP cookie or that unverified provider cookies do not exist. |
| Copyright & IP | Provenance, licences, permissions, marks, third-party assets and rights-notice process. | That attribution alone authorises copying or that the project owns event marks/artwork. |
| Disclaimer | Independent status, information freshness, external links and limits that preserve non-waivable rights. | A blanket waiver of mandatory rights or an organiser endorsement. |
| Accessibility Statement | Actual engineering target, known limitations, verified feedback channel and test status. | Certification or conformance based only on source inspection or automated scans. |
| Moderation & Reporting | Only real moderation operations, scope, intake, reviewer authority, privacy, appeal and escalation. | A monitored reporting service where no staffed channel exists. |\n| Child Safety & Protection | Feature-level child-access/risk assessment, data minimisation, safe external-link boundaries, age-related duties and truthful reporting limitations. | A child-safeguarding lead, active reporting mechanism, age-verification system, event admission rule or operator approval that does not exist. |
| Data Retention | Real category-specific retention periods, deletion, backup lifecycle and legal holds. | Invented periods where hosting and provider settings are unknown. |

## 3. Indonesia: applicability map

| Instrument | Main drafting/engineering relevance | Trigger and caution |
|---|---|---|
| UU 27/2022 Pelindungan Data Pribadi | Lawful basis, transparency, data-subject rights, minimisation, security, retention, breach response, processors and transfers. | Applies to personal-data processing within its territorial scope. Verify the controller and actual data flows. |
| PP 71/2019 | Electronic-system reliability, security, operational information and PSE duties. | Assess whether the actual operator/service is a private PSE and whether registration requirements apply. |
| UU 1/2024 (ITE amendment) | Electronic-system and child-protection provisions; online conduct and information obligations. | Read with the underlying ITE Law, implementing regulations and relevant Constitutional Court decisions. |
| PP 17/2025 | Governance of electronic systems for child protection. | Apply Article 3(3) and Article 4(2) feature-level assessment; distinguish a static information site from interactive features, but do not assume exemption. Record the indicators and evidence. |
| Permenkomdigi 9/2026 | Implements PP 17/2025: minimum age, age assurance, child-protection design, risk/self-assessment and social-networking provisions. | BPK record marks it in force from 6 March 2026. Determine product/service/feature class and risk before implementing age gates or verification. |
| UU 8/1999 and PP 80/2019 | Consumer information, unfair terms, seller identity, transactions and e-commerce. | Relevant if passes, goods, services, booking or payments are actually offered. |
| UU 28/2014 | Copyright, attribution, reproduction, adaptation, licensing and moral rights. | Review every copied text, image, logo, font, music, video and other third-party asset. |
| UU 8/2016 and PP 42/2020 | Disability rights and accessibility context. | Obtain a jurisdiction-specific assessment of duties applicable to the actual private website and event operator. |

### 3.1 PP 33/2026 status control

The repository's earlier research note states a 16 January 2027 commencement date but records that official BPK/peraturan.go.id publication verification was not completed. As of 24 November 2026, do not label it effective. Before relying on its specific provisions, verify the signed final text and Gazette/official repository record, then update the legal register.

## 4. International instruments — conditional, not universal

- **EU GDPR:** assess Article 3 territorial scope and the relevant processing basis. Article 8's child-consent age rule concerns consent under Article 6(1)(a) for information-society services offered directly to a child; it is not a universal global age threshold.
- **EU ePrivacy rules:** assess national implementation and actual cookie/device-storage behaviour.
- **EU Digital Services Act:** reassess if the project becomes an intermediary/hosting service or adds user-generated content, profiles, comments, uploads, messaging or marketplace features.
- **EU Accessibility Act:** assess listed service categories and territorial scope, especially if e-commerce, ticketing or booking is introduced.
- **UK GDPR, PECR and Age Appropriate Design Code:** assess establishment/targeting/monitoring and whether the service is likely to be accessed by children.
- **Singapore PDPA, Malaysia Act 709 and Thailand PDPA:** assess the relevant establishment, processing, commercial and territorial triggers; check amendments and current regulator guidance.
- **US COPPA:** assess whether the service is directed to children under 13 or has actual knowledge of collecting their personal information. Do not assume every child-facing website is automatically a COPPA-covered operator.
- **California CCPA/CPRA and other US state laws:** assess business/entity and threshold tests, data types, purposes and current state-specific applicability.

## 5. Child safety and safeguarding

Before any child-facing interactive feature is added:
- complete a documented age/risk assessment and service classification;
- use age assurance only where required and proportionate, privacy-preserving and non-discriminatory;
- minimise collection and avoid collecting precise location, identity documents, health details or biometric data without a demonstrated necessity and lawful basis;
- define safe contact boundaries, block/mute controls where relevant, reporting and escalation;
- prohibit grooming, coercion, sexual exploitation, doxxing, harassment and retaliation;
- establish trained and authorised safeguarding responders and secure evidence handling;
- publish age-appropriate, accessible notices and clear parent/guardian information where legally required;
- never advertise a reporting channel until it is actually monitored.

## 6. CoC procedural fairness baseline

Any future operative CoC should specify:
1. Exact spaces, people and activities in scope.
2. Plain-language expected conduct and prohibited conduct.
3. Consent and recording/media boundaries.
4. Child-safeguarding and power-imbalance protections.
5. Reporting channel, evidence handling and confidentiality limits.
6. Triage and emergency escalation boundaries.
7. Conflict-of-interest management and impartial review.
8. Opportunity to respond, proportionate measures and written reasons.
9. Appeal or reconsideration path and anti-retaliation safeguards.
10. Policy owner, version, effective date, review schedule and accessible translations.

## 7. Approval checklist

- [ ] Actual legal operator and policy owner identified.
- [ ] Service model and jurisdictional triggers documented.
- [ ] Data inventory and vendor/transfer map verified.
- [ ] Child-access and risk classification completed.
- [ ] Terms reviewed against actual transactions and mandatory consumer rights.
- [ ] CoC scope and moderation operations verified.
- [ ] Copyright and media permissions recorded.
- [ ] Retention periods match real systems and provider contracts.
- [ ] Complete language versions reviewed by competent translators.
- [ ] Qualified counsel review completed.
- [ ] Operator formally approves version and effective date.
- [ ] Public contact and reporting channels tested before publication.

Until every applicable gate is satisfied, retain the visible drafting-only banner and do not present any draft as binding.
