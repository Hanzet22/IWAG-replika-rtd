# V2 child-access and PP 17/2025 scope assessment

**Assessment date:** 25 November 2026  
**Repository baseline:** `main` immediately before this research branch  
**Status:** Internal legal-scope assessment — provisional — not a legal opinion or regulator determination

## 1. Decision summary

V2 must not conclude that Indonesian child-protection rules are irrelevant merely because the current site has no accounts, comments or user uploads.

**Government Regulation No. 17 of 2025 (PP 17/2025)** defines the relevant scope by whether an electronic-system provider develops or operates products, services or features specifically designed for, or that may be used or accessed by, children. The “may be accessed” category is assessed using the indicators in **Pasal 4 ayat (2) huruf a–e**. Those indicators include the operator's published terms/policies, evidence about the actual user population, advertising, design elements and substantial similarity to services already shown to be used by children.

V2 is a public event-information and educational reference website. It has no accounts, registration, payment, messaging, public comments, user uploads, community feed or intentionally configured advertising. This reduces several interaction and data risks, but it does not itself decide whether the service may be accessed by children. The audience composition is not verified, and the subject matter/design may attract younger users. Therefore the legal scope remains **open pending a documented operator/audience assessment**.

Do not introduce blanket age verification or parental gates solely as a precaution without determining the statutory trigger, proportionality and final implementing requirements. Equally, do not publish a categorical claim that PP 17/2025 does not apply.

- **Type/number/year:** Peraturan Pemerintah (PP) No. 17 Tahun 2025.
- **Gazette:** Lembaran Negara Republik Indonesia Tahun 2025 Nomor 36; Tambahan Lembaran Negara Nomor 7105.
- **Promulgated and effective:** 27 March 2025; JDIH BPK marks the instrument as in force.
- **Official metadata:** https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/316698/pp-no-17-tahun-2025
- **Official text:** https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/unduh/id/965/t/peraturan+pemerintah+nomor+17+tahun+2025 and https://www.peraturan.go.id/files/pp-no-17-tahun-2025.pdf

Relevant pinpoint provisions:

| Provision | Legal meaning for the scope review |
|---|---|
| Pasal 1 angka 1–7 | Defines child, electronic system/provider, product/service/feature, personal data and child-data impact assessment. |
| Pasal 2 ayat (1)–(4) | Establishes child-protection duties for electronic systems and requires age-limit information, child-user verification and a reporting mechanism within the stated scope. |
| Pasal 3 ayat (3) | Private PSE scope includes products/services/features specifically designed for, or that may be used or accessed by, children. |
| Pasal 4 ayat (1) huruf a–b and ayat (2) huruf a–e | Separates child-designed services from services that may be accessed by children and gives the indicators used to assess the latter. |
| Pasal 4 ayat (3) | Further indicators are to be set by Ministerial Decision. The relevant final ministerial instrument must be verified before applying an indicator checklist as exhaustive. |
| Pasal 5 ayat (1)–(9) | Requires risk assessment; considers contact with strangers, harmful content, child-consumer exploitation, child-data security, addiction, psychological health and physiological health; requires self-assessment and reporting/verification steps. |
| Pasal 7 ayat (1) huruf a–j, ayat (2) huruf a–c and ayat (3) | Lists child-protection duties and prohibitions, including parental/guardian authorisation, child-data impact assessment, high-privacy defaults, understandable information, age-appropriate functions, and prohibitions on covert practices, precise child geolocation collection and child profiling. |
| Pasal 8 | Requires best interests of the child and prioritisation of child rights/protection over commercial interests. |
| Pasal 9 ayat (1)–(5) | Sets parental/guardian consent and related consequences for a child using an in-scope product/service/feature. Do not silently convert this into a universal age gate without determining scope and the implementing rules. |
| Pasal 10–11 | Addresses high-privacy defaults and information for users. |
| Pasal 19 | Restricts profiling of children, subject to its stated exceptions. |

| Pasal 23 ayat (1)–(2) | Requires accessible reporting tools and operational follow-up for child-rights problems within scope. |

**Locator note:** The official PDF uses Pasal/ayat/huruf/angka and printed page numbering. No stable statutory “line number” is published. Do not invent one. The official text places the scope provisions around printed pages 3–6, duties around pages 6–10 and reporting around pages 15–16; use these page references only as navigation aids, not as substitutes for the pinpoint provisions.

## 4. Data-storage facts found in source

The preference script writes only user-selected display/accessibility preferences to browser local storage. The seven keys currently identified are:

- `iwag-v2-theme`
- `iwag-v2-accent`
- `iwag-v2-text-scale`
- `iwag-v2-text-spacing`
- `iwag-v2-contrast`
- `iwag-v2-motion`
- `iwag-v2-density`

The reviewed preference script does not send these values to an analytics service or the application server. Local storage is not an HTTP cookie and is not automatically included in HTTP requests. This does **not** establish what Vercel, DNS, CDN, security or other infrastructure providers log independently. Those provider data flows, locations, retention periods and subprocessors remain unverified.

## 6. Decision and actions before policy approval

1. Confirm the legal operator/controller and who can approve policies.
2. Record the intended audience and the actual audience evidence available without adding intrusive analytics.
3. Review the V2 public-facing terms, navigation, visual design, event/fandom content and external links against every PP 17/2025 Pasal 4 ayat (2) indicator.
4. Complete a documented self-assessment under Pasal 5 if the product/service/feature is within scope; retain the evidence and rationale.
5. Obtain the final gazetted Permenkomdigi 9/2026 and any Ministerial Decision setting Pasal 4 ayat (3) indicators before finalising a compliance determination.
6. Keep the current site read-only unless and until the operator has implemented the controls required for the actual risk classification.
7. If an in-scope child-facing feature is proposed, assess parental/guardian authorisation, privacy by default, age assurance, reporting, moderation, vendor duties, DPIA and DPO obligations before release.
8. Do not use an event organiser's age rule as a substitute for the website's own statutory scope analysis.

## 7. Other child-safety regimes — feature and territorial triggers

- **EU:** GDPR Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Article 8 applies to consent-based processing in the context of an information-society service offered directly to a child; the age is set by each Member State between 13 and 16. It is not a universal minimum age for reading public pages or for every lawful basis.
- **United Kingdom:** Data Protection Act 2018, section 123 authorises the Age Appropriate Design Code for relevant information-society services likely to be accessed by children and processing personal data. ICO guidance includes many news, educational and content websites; whether a free non-commercial independent project is an information-society service is fact-specific.
- **United States:** COPPA, 15 U.S.C. §§6501–6506 and 16 C.F.R. Part 312, applies to child-directed commercial services or general-audience operators with actual knowledge of collection from children under 13. The 2025 Rule amendments expressly include passive tracking and IP addresses among relevant identifiers; the internal-operations exception is narrow.
- **Singapore:** PDPC's 28 March 2024 Advisory Guidelines on Children's Personal Data in the Digital Environment cover organisations whose online products/services are likely to be accessed by children, but are guidance explaining the PDPA, not a standalone statute.
- **India:** Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, Act 22 of 2023, includes a child under 18 in section 2 and child-specific duties in section 9, but the main operational provisions including section 9 are scheduled for commencement 18 months after 13 November 2025 (13 May 2027). Do not describe section 9 as already in force on 25 November 2026.
- **Brazil:** LGPD, Law No. 13,709/2018, Article 14 governs processing of children's and adolescents' personal data, with best-interests requirements. Its territorial scope is separately determined under Article 3.
- **South Korea:** Personal Information Protection Act, Act No. 10465 of 2011 as amended, including Article 22-2 on personal information of children under 14, subject to its Korean territorial/application rules.

These regimes do not create one worldwide age threshold. Each must be assessed against the actual operator, service, data processing, audience and territorial trigger.

## 8. Limits

This is a source-based engineering/legal-scope assessment, not a legal opinion. It does not establish that V2 is in or out of scope under every national law, nor does it replace an official regulator determination or advice from Indonesian counsel. Reassess whenever the audience, content, tracking, advertising, accounts, UGC, messaging, ticketing, payments or operator changes.
