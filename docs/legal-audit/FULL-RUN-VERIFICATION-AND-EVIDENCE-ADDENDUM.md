# IWAG V2 — Full-Run Legal Verification & Evidence Addendum

**Audit date:** 25 November 2026  
**Status:** Research and drafting support only — not a legal opinion, compliance certification, or organiser-approved policy.

## 1. Executive disposition

This pass confirms several legal-status facts, identifies one critical publication defect in the V2 preview, and deepens the evidence base through full-text review of six scholarly papers.

- **Permenkomdigi No. 9/2026:** official catalogues identify an instrument promulgated 6 March 2026, BN 2026 No. 163, but the downloadable government-linked PDF is still headed `RANCANGAN`, contains promulgation placeholders, and has an incomplete Article 12. The exact final gazetted wording remains **unverified**. Do not cite its draft article text as operative law.
- **PP No. 33/2026:** available copies report promulgation 16 July 2026, LN 2026 No. 88/TLN No. 7190, and commencement six months later, 16 January 2027. The official central gazette copy was not authenticated in this pass. Treat the date and article-level detail as corroborated but subject to gazette verification.
- **PP No. 17/2025:** in force from 27 March 2025. The two-year adjustment period ends 27 March 2027. Child-accessible electronic products/services/features need a scope and risk assessment; a static information page is lower risk than registration, ticketing, messaging, profiling, or account features, but “read-only” is not a categorical exemption.
- **PP No. 60/2017 + Perpol No. 7/2023:** event-permit classification, application lead times, documents, competent authority, and foreign-person involvement must be assessed against the actual event and venue. Application deadlines are not the same as permit issuance periods.
- **GitHub:** PR #11 had removed the explicit non-official/draft warning from legal pages. That could mislead visitors into treating an independent reconstruction as an adopted IWAG rulebook. The warning is restored in this stack.
- **Research evidence:** full-text reading supports whole-journey accessibility, early multi-agency preparedness, locally validated medical planning, and clear-but-balanced moderation. These are evidence-informed safeguards, not statutory provisions.

## 2. Indonesian legal verification

### 2.1 Permenkomdigi No. 9 Tahun 2026 — source-integrity anomaly

Government catalogues:
- Directorate General of Legislation: https://peraturan.go.id/id/permenkomdigi-no-9-tahun-2026
- Komdigi JDIH: https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/view/id/1007/t/peraturan+menteri+komunikasi+dan+digital+nomor+9+tahun+2026
- BPK metadata: https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/346040/permenkomdigi-no-9-tahun-2026

These records identify Permenkomdigi 9/2026 as promulgated 6 March 2026, Berita Negara 2026 No. 163, and in force. However, the downloadable BPK/JDIH PDFs inspected are marked **RANCANGAN**, leave promulgation and BN fields as placeholders, and contain an incomplete Article 12 (including an ellipsis). Article 63 in that file says it commences on promulgation, but a draft's commencement clause does not authenticate its final text.

**Disposition:** record the metadata, but do not quote or rely on draft article numbering or text as the final regulation. Obtain an authenticated BN 2026 No. 163 copy or written clarification from the competent JDIH. Any article-specific Permenkomdigi conclusion in this project remains provisional until that source is obtained.

### 2.2 PP No. 33 Tahun 2026 — future commencement

Available copy: http://ppid.purbalinggakab.go.id/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/peraturan_pemerintah_no._33_tahun_2026_100926115902.pdf  
Additional available copy used for text discovery: https://legaltrans.id/downloads/pp-33-tahun-2026-pelindungan-data-pribadi.pdf

The available texts report promulgation on 16 July 2026, LN 2026 No. 88, TLN No. 7190, with Article 225 providing commencement six months after promulgation: **16 January 2027**. Because an authenticated central gazette copy was not obtained in this pass, preserve the status label **government/secondary copy corroboration — central gazette verification open**.

The available text includes provisions relevant to controller governance and international transfers, including Articles 162 and 164–165. Do not quote exact wording or present article-level interpretation as finally authenticated until the official gazette text is checked.

**Temporal rule:** as of 25 November 2026, UU No. 27/2022 remains the operative PDP baseline. PP 33/2026 is enacted according to the available records but not yet commenced. Existing UU PDP duties cannot be postponed to January 2027.

### 2.3 PP No. 17 Tahun 2025 — child protection in electronic systems

Official JDIH copy: https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/unduh/id/965/t/peraturan%2Bpemerintah%2Bnomor%2B17%2Btahun%2B2025

PP 17/2025 was promulgated and took effect on 27 March 2025. Its two-year adjustment period ends **27 March 2027**. It addresses electronic-system products, services, and features designed for children or that may be accessed by children. The assessment should consider the product/service as actually designed and used, not merely its marketing label.

For IWAG:
- A genuinely static public-information page has a different risk profile from registration, ticketing, accounts, attendee profiles, direct messaging, user submissions, precise location, targeted marketing, or behavioural analytics.
- Child access still requires a documented scope and risk assessment; do not claim an automatic “read-only” exemption.
- A third-party platform or ticketing provider does not automatically eliminate organiser/controller duties. Determine who decides purposes and means, what data each party receives, and what contracts say.
- Do not reduce the regulation to a universal parental-click-box rule for every person under 18. Age bands, service features, data types, legal bases, and the authenticated implementing text matter.

### 2.4 PP No. 60 Tahun 2017 and Perpol No. 7 Tahun 2023 — public-event permits

Primary/official references:
- PP 60/2017 text: https://peraturan.go.id/files/pp60-2017bt.pdf
- PP 60/2017 record: https://peraturan.go.id/id/pp-no-60-tahun-2017
- Perpol 7/2023 BPK record: https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/326714/peraturan-polri-no-7-tahun-2023
- Perpol 7/2023 police legal-library copy: https://sisdivkum.id/storage/library/file/0645-PERATURAN_KEPOLISIAN_NEGARA_REPUBLIK_INDONESIA.pdf

Key distinctions recorded by the verification pass:
- PP 60 Article 3 covers public crowd activities, public performances, and processions on public roads; Article 4 captures other community activities capable of endangering public security.
- PP 60 Article 6 and Perpol 7 Article 12(7) provide application lead times of at least **14 working days** (ordinary/regional), **21 working days** (national), or **30 working days** (international), subject to the applicable route and classification.
- PP 60 Article 9 concerns permit issuance periods after a complete application and coordination: up to **4 / 7 / 14 working days** by scale. These are not the same as the application lead times.
- PP 60 Article 7 requires the purpose/nature, place/time, expected participants, responsible person, committee, venue-holder approval, relevant recommendations, and a written compliance declaration.
- Perpol 7 adds technical documents and administrative requirements, including venue permission, committee information, recommendations, and passport/visa copies where foreign persons are involved.
- Perpol 7's examples include festivals, bazaars, exhibitions, sport, concerts, contests, parades, and activities involving foreigners. The exact classification and competent police office must be confirmed with the relevant police authority.

**Foreign-person caution:** foreign involvement may affect classification and documentation, but do not write “one foreign attendee automatically makes the event international.” Distinguish foreign organiser, speaker, performer, worker/contractor, exhibitor/dealer, formal delegate, and ordinary attendee. Confirm facts and obtain police/immigration advice.

## 3. Full-text scholarly evidence

These papers inform operational design; none creates Indonesian legal obligations. Their settings and methods limit transferability.

### 3.1 Accessibility — Jiménez-Andres (2024), [Jim24]

*Accessibility of large events: an empirical study of the Expo 2020 Dubai.* DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10209-023-01079-7

- Exploratory qualitative case study using observation, questionnaires, interviews, and review of online accessibility information.
- Small sample: 4 in-depth interviews with disabled participants, 7 questionnaire respondents, 3 accessibility consultants, 2 family members, and 3 staff; one approximately six-hour observation visit.
- Participants valued mobility aids, fast-track access, staff assistance, and some accessible infrastructure.
- Access was uneven: heavy doors, inaccessible toilet doors, obstructed wheelchair routes, missed exhibition content on alternative routes, displays out of reach, and advertised services that staff could not reliably explain or provide.
- The central lesson is that entry/accessibility claims do not guarantee autonomous participation through navigation, toilets, exhibits, communication, and service delivery.
- **Convention controls:** involve disabled users/accessibility expertise early; bind exhibitors and vendors to access requirements; inspect the full journey; test toilets, doors, routes, booth/display reach, information and staff knowledge; provide clear accommodation contacts.
- **Limitations:** small UAE sample, two of eleven organisations participated, one observation session, and COVID-era constraints. Not a representative estimate or a tested convention intervention.

### 3.2 Medical planning — van Remoortel et al. (2020), [Rem20]

*Prediction modelling studies for medical usage rates in mass gatherings: A systematic review.* DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0234977

- Systematic review of 16 studies: 13 model-development and 3 application studies; more than 1,700 gatherings and 48 million attendees.
- Overall GRADE certainty was low for patient presentation, hospital transfer, and injury outcomes; risk of bias and indirectness were substantial.
- Reported model performance varied. Cross-context applications produced errors from 22% overestimation to 81% underestimation.
- Biomedical and environmental predictors included event type, crowd size, accommodation, weather, water availability, time, demographics, and activity type. No psychosocial predictors were included in the reviewed models.
- **Convention controls:** do not copy a universal first-aid staffing ratio or rely on attendance alone. Use venue, activity, duration, climate, water access, attendee profile, local emergency access, and prior local event data; validate assumptions with local medical/venue professionals.
- **Limitations:** heterogeneous studies, mostly US/Australian evidence, no robust large-dataset external validation, and no meta-analysis.

### 3.3 Emergency preparedness — Koski, Kouvonen & Sumanen (2020), [Kos20]

*Preparedness for Mass Gatherings: Factors to Consider According to the Rescue Authorities.* DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17041361

- Qualitative interview study of 15 Finnish rescue-authority specialists; 11 hours of interviews, analysed through inductive content analysis.
- Three lifecycle categories: pre-planning cooperation, emergency-plan content, and actions during the event.
- Authorities emphasized early multi-agency planning, organiser competence, learning from previous events, attendee/event characteristics, venue infrastructure, weather/disruptions, crowd flow and evacuation, authority access, communications, and a shared operational picture.
- **Convention controls:** map incident command and responsibilities; plan with venue, police, EMS/rescue, security and relevant suppliers; inspect the actual building and routes; maintain clear egress and responder access; prepare communications and scenario exercises.
- **Limitations:** Finnish qualitative study, not an intervention trial or indoor-convention study. Use as a planning framework, not a binding standard or a numerical checklist.

### 3.4 Moderation explanations — Jhaver et al. (2019), [Jha19b]

*Does Transparency in Moderation Really Matter? User Behavior After Content Removal Explanations on Reddit.* DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3359252

- Observational analysis of approximately 32.3 million Reddit submissions and 212,748 identified removal explanations.
- Higher explanation rates were associated with lower odds of future post removal (OR 0.935 per standard-deviation increase in one model).
- The relationship with future posting was mixed; one model associated more explanations with lower odds of posting. Comment explanations and bot explanations behaved differently across specifications.
- **Convention controls:** explain the relevant rule, factual basis at an appropriate level, what the person can do next, and how to request review; use templates only when accurate; offer human review for ambiguous cases; monitor both repeat violations and unjustified participation deterrence.
- **Limitations:** observational, not causal; private messages unavailable; explanations may not have been read; appeals and decision correctness were not evaluated.

### 3.5 Moderation trade-offs — Jiang et al. (2022), [Jia22]

*A Trade-off-centered Framework of Content Moderation.* DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3534929

- Systematic review of 86 empirical content-moderation papers.
- Organises moderation choices into actions, styles, philosophies, and values, including exclusion versus participation, human versus automated, centralised versus distributed, transparent versus opaque, nurturing versus punitive, and safety versus expression.
- Transparency can aid understanding and legitimacy, but can also reveal evasion opportunities, expose moderators, create conflict, and add workload. Automation can scale consistency but also scale errors.
- **Convention controls:** distinguish immediate safety interventions from final sanctions; use proportional actions; document rationale; protect reporter privacy; provide a meaningful review route; avoid pretending one process optimises every stakeholder interest.
- **Limitations:** literature is weighted toward Reddit and volunteer moderation; limited commercial moderation evidence; not a live-event governance study.

### 3.6 Community rules and governance perceptions — Leibmann et al. (2025), [Lei25]

*Reddit Rules and Rulers: Quantifying the Link Between Rules and Perceptions of Governance across Thousands of Communities.* DOI: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.14163

- Analysis of 67,545 rules across 5,225 Reddit communities and 3.8 million governance-related posts/comments.
- User-related rules were associated with 1.83 percentage points more positive governance-related discussion after adjustment.
- Across 6,645 rule additions, positive sentiment rose 0.61 percentage points and negative sentiment fell 0.77 percentage points immediately after addition; effects were no longer significant after about six months.
- **Convention controls:** use specific, understandable, constructive rules and review them over time; do not infer that adding more rules or changing wording alone causes better governance.
- **Limitations:** observational, English-language Reddit rules, visibility/enforcement not measured, residual confounding, and no event-specific test.

## 3.7 Additional evidence leads from the completed 2026 literature search (abstract-level review)

The completed Undermind search surfaced 228 relevant records. The following items were reviewed at abstract/metadata level in this pass, **not full-text read**. They are research leads and should not be described as independently validated full-text findings.

- **Waltz et al. (2026), [Wal26b]**, *Making festivals accessible: a report from the Revelland project*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1108/ijefm-12-2025-0205. Interviews with festival accessibility staff and observations/checklists across six festivals in 2024–2025 identify barriers in information, ticketing, travel support, physical facilities and technology; staff knowledge was central to inclusion. Useful for a practical whole-journey audit, but the available abstract does not establish effect sizes.
- **Al Bochi et al. (2023), [Boc23]**, *Evacuation Solutions for Individuals with Functional Limitations in the Indoor Built Environment: A Scoping Review*, DOI https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings13112779. The review screened 3,562 records and included 99 studies, grouping solutions into notification, wayfinding, egress, building design, strategy and training. This supports planning for disabled attendees in evacuation, but does not replace venue-specific fire-engineering or legal review.
- **Castle et al. (2022), [Cas22]**, *Attending live music events with a visual impairment*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1108/aam-04-2022-0015. Mixed-methods study with 20 interviews and 94 survey responses identifies barriers in event information/ticketing, staff, navigation, facilities and specialist services. Useful as an accessibility checklist; not a convention-specific sample.
- **Martinez et al. (2026), [Mar26]**, *Preparedness for sexual violence at mass gatherings*, DOI https://doi.org/10.5055/ajdm.0532. Scoping review of 16 studies reports under-recognition/under-reporting and identifies trauma-informed staff preparation, private support spaces and referral pathways as operational themes. The available abstract is not sufficient to assess intervention effectiveness or transfer directly to an Indonesian convention.
- **Assini-Meytin et al. (2020), [Ass20]**, *Preventing and responding to child sexual abuse: Organizational efforts*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chiabu.2020.104892. Document analysis of 74 policies and related documents from four US youth-serving organisations identified safeguarding domains including codes, training, screening, monitoring and response. It indexes policy domains; it does not test their effectiveness or determine Indonesian legal duties.
- **Lee et al. (2025), [Lee25]**, *Mapping Community Appeals Systems*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3757627. Qualitative study of 17 Discord community moderators describes community appeals practices and perceived trade-offs in fairness, accountability and community values. This is useful as an implementation case, not proof that a particular appeal model is effective.
- **Shim & Jhaver (2024), [Shi24]**, *Incorporating Procedural Fairness in Flag Submissions on Social Media Platforms*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3797820. Survey experiment with 2,936 US respondents found that displaying community guidelines improved perceived transparency and an open-text field improved perceived voice. It measures perceptions in an online reporting interface, not event incident outcomes.
- **Ribeiro et al. (2024), [Rib24]**, *Post Guidance for Online Communities*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3711046. Randomized field experiment involving 97,616 posters across 33 subreddits over 63 days found proactive guidance increased successful posts and reduced moderator workload. This supports clear pre-submission guidance in online communities; it is not evidence about physical event reporting.
- **Dym & Fiesler (2020), [Dym20b]**, *Social Norm Vulnerability and its Consequences for Privacy and Safety in an Online Community*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3415226. Interview study with 25 transformative-fandom participants describes how community norms can fail when sub-community tensions, outsider participation and platform design conflict. Particularly relevant to linked fandom spaces, but the findings are qualitative and not a legal standard.
- **Fileborn et al. (2020), [Fil20]**, *Sexual harassment and violence at Australian music festivals*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1177/0004865820903777. Sixteen in-depth interviews describe barriers to reporting in festival settings. The small Australian sample provides context, not a prevalence estimate for IWAG.

**Use rule:** only the six studies in Sections 3.1–3.6 were full-text read in this run. This subsection is explicitly abstract-level discovery and should not be conflated with full-text appraisal.

## 4. Evidence-based operational control map

| Area | Evidence-informed control | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Accessibility | Full-journey audit, early disabled-user input, exhibitor requirements, accessible toilets/routes/displays, trained staff | Paper evidence is qualitative and does not establish a universal technical standard |
| Event safety | Venue-specific risk register, multi-agency planning, evacuation/responder access, comms, scenario exercises | Finnish authority study is not Indonesian law |
| Medical readiness | Local data and context-specific planning; avoid universal ratios | Prediction evidence is low certainty |
| Harassment response | Multiple reporting routes, trained responders, private support, referral pathways, anti-retaliation | Evidence does not guarantee a specific intervention effect |
| Moderation | Rule-specific reasons, proportionate actions, review, privacy, human escalation | Explanations/appeals are not directly tested in every event setting |
| Governance | Reassess rules and outcomes; monitor both safety and participation | Community-perception studies are observational |

## 5. Critical repository correction

PR #11 had removed the explicit status banner and replaced it with a version line. The page still called itself an independent project, but that did not adequately communicate that the policy content was **not official, not adopted, and not in force**. Because these pages reconstruct event rules, that distinction is material.

The status banner is restored in the updated PR #11 / PR #12 stack in English and Indonesian. The banner states that the material is an independent draft, not official IWAG policy, not approved by the organiser, and not in force. It also warns readers not to rely on it as an operative event rule.

- Do not claim worldwide or complete legal compliance.
