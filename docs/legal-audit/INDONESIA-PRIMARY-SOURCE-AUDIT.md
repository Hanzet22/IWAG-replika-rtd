# IWAG V2 — Indonesia Primary-Source Legal Audit and Public Operating-Facts Map

**As of:** 25 November 2026  
**Research branch:** `research/global-legal-compliance-audit`  
**Scope:** IWAG's currently published website and 2026/2027 event information, Indonesian national law, conditional local law, and the legal controls that should be reflected in V2.

> This is a research and issue-spotting document, not a legal opinion or compliance certification. It distinguishes the law's text from conclusions about whether it applies to IWAG. Final applicability depends on the actual contracting organiser, legal form, NIB/OSS activity, platform roles, venue, event format, attendee origins, vendors, contracts, data flows and actual operations. A qualified Indonesian lawyer and relevant authorities should review the final operating model.

## 1. Method and evidence labels

- **Primary text inspected:** the legal instrument or official government record was retrieved and relevant provisions were read.
- **Official record inspected:** an official BPK/JDIH/OSS record was read for title, status, date or metadata; the entire underlying law may still need detailed analysis.
- **Government-hosted copy:** a government site hosts a copy, but it is not the central promulgating authority's canonical record.
- **Secondary / corroboration:** useful for discovery, but not controlling legal authority.
- **Open:** exact applicability, amendment status, official record or operating fact still needs confirmation.

The audit date is 25 November 2026. Future-effective rules are not labelled as already in force.

## 2. IWAG public operating facts discovered

These facts come from IWAG's own public pages. They are evidence of published representations, not proof that every activity occurred exactly as described or that the same arrangements will apply in 2027.

### 2.1 Identity, event location and legal form

- IWAG describes itself as an Indonesian furry convention in Greater Jakarta: https://iwag.furries.id/en/
- The Privacy Policy describes “Staff” as the steering committee responsible for the platform and event and says the staff is a **non-corporate social organization located in Jakarta, Indonesia**: https://iwag.furries.id/en/privacy
- The page does not clearly identify the exact legal person/association, its registration status, the contracting party, the authorised signatory, its tax identity, or the individual/entity responsible for consumer contracts and personal-data-controller duties.
- IWAG's 2026 hotel information identifies ibis Styles Serpong BSD City as the host hotel for that event and describes group reservations handled through IWAG: https://iwag.furries.id/en/updates/2026-01-05-hotel-information
- IWAG's official venue announcement dated 15 November 2026 identifies **Hotel Ciputra Jakarta, West Jakarta**, for **3–4 July 2027**: https://iwag.furries.id/en/updates/2026-11-15-venue-reveal/ . The 2026 ibis Styles Serpong BSD City hotel is historical context only; apply DKI Jakarta/Jakarta Barat screening to the 2027 venue.

**Legal consequence:** identify the actual contracting organiser and authorised representatives before deciding licensing, consumer, tax, privacy-controller, liability and dispute obligations. A description such as “non-corporate social organization” is not enough to determine legal personality or who signs contracts.

### 2.2 Published activities and commercial features

The About page describes workshops, talk shows, games and art exhibitions: https://iwag.furries.id/en/about

IWAG's 2026 panel/activity notice solicited entertainment, social and educational activities, including live performances, watch parties, workshops, demonstrations and talks: https://iwag.furries.id/en/updates/2026-01-13-panel-registration

The 2026 Dealers Den notice allowed applications for physical or digital artistic goods/merchandise, commissions, convention/community representation and display-only works. It said the table was free and that food/beverage sales were not allowed in Dealers Den: https://iwag.furries.id/en/updates/2026-01-28-dealers

The 2026 conclusion page says Art Show and Fureats Café were introduced that year. Therefore, “no food sales in Dealers Den” must not be misread as proof that no food/beverage service existed anywhere at the event: https://iwag.furries.id/en/updates/2026-07-14-conclusion

**Legal consequence:** screen permits, business activity classification, consumer disclosures, vendor agreements, product safety, tax, copyright/music/performance licensing, and food hygiene only for activities actually offered. Reconfirm the 2027 programme before applying the 2026 facts.

### 2.3 Hotel booking and payments

The 2026 hotel page says IWAG reserved room inventory, collected reservation forms, checked availability with the hotel, issued invoices/payment instructions, confirmed payments and later sent booking details to attendees. The page also refers to group booking and booking batches sent to the hotel.

The 2027 pass page advertises hotel-booking priority and hotel welcome amenities for a pass tier, but the actual 2027 hotel and contractual arrangement were not yet established in the pages inspected.

**Legal consequence:** determine whether IWAG acts as the hotel’s disclosed booking agent, an intermediary, a reseller, or a separate contracting party; identify who receives the room payment, issues invoices, handles cancellation/refund, bears overbooking risk, and controls the booking data. The hotel and organiser may be separate controllers for different purposes. Do not draft the hotel terms until the hotel agreement and payment flow are reviewed.

### 2.4 Registration, identity verification and age rules

The current CoC says passes are obtained through the Furries.id Community platform and that attendees must show an event pass and physical legal ID to receive a badge. It says ID details are visually checked and not retained except for serious breaches, fraud or lawful purposes: https://iwag.furries.id/en/coc

The CoC's published age rules include:
- general event audience described as 14 and above;
- attendees aged 14–16 require parental consent and photo ID;
- under-14 attendance is nevertheless allowed under separate rules;
- ages 11–13 require a pass and a legal guardian;
- ages 10 and below may register at the door with an accompanying adult holding a pass;
- official programming is described as suitable for ages 14+ with parental discretion/waiver for younger attendees.

These provisions need a clear, consistent age table and a safeguarding procedure. They are not interchangeable with the separate question of whether the online platform is a regulated child-accessible electronic system.

**Legal consequence:** map which entity collects full legal name, date of birth, ID attributes, guardian details and pass records; what the physical ID check records; whether ID images are ever stored; what happens to records of minors; and how consent/guardian authority is verified. Minimise identity data and separate a visual check from retention of a copy.

### 2.5 Privacy notice and data flows

The current Privacy Policy expressly lists:
- full official name from government ID;
- date of birth from government ID;
- email;
- payment reconciliation data from third-party payment providers, including payment ID, funding method, status, chargeback/reversal/claim/void information, timestamps and platform/payment-provider logs;
- optional phone number;
- optional third-party personal data about family/friends/other people;
- optional health conditions and emergency contacts for safety;
- authorised staff photography, filming and voice recording for publication or security;
- possible sharing of personal data for violations and lawful authority requests.

It also states that it does not store certain direct financial credentials such as account/card numbers, CVC/CVV, OTP/PIN or authentication tokens. This is a useful minimisation statement, but does not remove the need to document payment-provider roles and data flows.

The notice describes withdrawal of voluntarily provided PII by written request and says PII will be revoked with written confirmation. It does not clearly describe all retention exceptions, statutory accounting retention, litigation/claims holds, backups, vendor retention, or how erasure requests are handled across processors.

**Material gaps observed on the public notice:**
- no clear named controller/legal person beyond “Staff” and a non-corporate social organisation description;
- no clear list of processors/recipients (payment provider, platform, Google Forms/Drive, email, hosting, hotel, media vendors);
- no hosting/storage region or cross-border transfer explanation;
- no child-specific privacy notice or clear parent/guardian consent process;
- no clear data retention schedule by data category;
- no detailed distinction between health data, emergency contacts, identity verification and payment reconciliation;
- no clear breach response timeline or operational contact for data incidents;
- no precise mapping of purposes to lawful bases;
- no clear account of public photo/video contribution folders and their sharing/retention controls.

Source: https://iwag.furries.id/en/privacy

### 2.6 Photography, video and public sharing

IWAG's 2026 conclusion page invited attendees to upload photos/videos to a publicly accessible Google Drive folder. It instructed contributors to obtain consent where identifiable non-fursuit faces appear in the foreground, prohibited uploads from private spaces without permission, and linked to staff-curated photos and YouTube recordings: https://iwag.furries.id/en/updates/2026-07-14-conclusion

The CoC also requires consent for personal photos/videos, restricts photography in private/sensitive spaces and requires prior written approval for commercial photography, journalism, mass media and livestreaming.

**Legal consequence:** map who is responsible for the public folder, who can access it, whether contributors are told that uploads become public, how takedown requests work, how minors are handled, how long content remains online, and whether Google/YouTube are processors or independent controllers for relevant operations. Do not assume storage location from the brand name; verify contract, region and transfer terms.

### 2.7 Conduct, accessibility and attendee remedy

The CoC covers event spaces, venue public areas and hotel spaces; it applies to attendees, panelists, performers, dealers, media/press and some venue patrons. It includes harassment, discrimination, stalking, unwelcome contact, threats, violence, venue rules, fursuit boundaries, photography and hotel quiet hours.

The CoC does not contain a clearly developed disability-accessibility/accommodation section. The public page also uses broad discretionary categories such as “immoral” or “socially unacceptable” items and “inappropriately sexual” dressing without fully objective criteria. Its sanctions include badge revocation, expulsion, future bans, financial liability/fines and police involvement, but the public process does not clearly specify notice, evidence handling, decision-maker independence, written reasons, appeal timetable or emergency exceptions.

**Legal consequence:** add accessibility and reasonable-accommodation pathways; define restricted categories objectively; distinguish immediate safety interventions from final sanctions; preserve evidence and privacy; provide accessible reporting, reasons and a meaningful review route; do not promise a private event process that obstructs lawful reporting to authorities.

### 2.8 Ticketing terms and consumer-risk clauses

The current T&C says:
- “All sales are final” and no voluntary refunds;
- attendee cancellation, non-attendance, downgrade or removal of PII does not generate a refund;
- force-majeure refunds are determined by the Event;
- no-show attendees receive no refund and no guaranteed compensation for unclaimed perks;
- for rescheduling, alteration, moving or cancellation outside force majeure, staff says it is not responsible for attendees' travel, accommodation and related costs;
- broad liability waiver, except liabilities mandated by Indonesian law;
- no detailed court jurisdiction/arbitration/dispute escalation clause was identified in the reviewed text; support email is the stated contact route.

Source: https://iwag.furries.id/en/tnc

**Legal consequence:** these are high-priority review points under Indonesian consumer law, especially rules on standard clauses and exclusion/transfer of responsibility. Do not assume the “all sales final” label overrides statutory consumer rights. The final V2 should distinguish attendee cancellation, organiser cancellation, postponement, material change, venue unavailability, force majeure, partial service delivery, payment-provider failure and hotel booking cancellation.

### 2.9 Broken/uncertain public help link

The T&C refers to the Registration FAQ for pass-transfer details. A current scrape of the mapped English route returned a page-not-found response. Confirm the canonical route and repair the link or restore the page before relying on it as part of contractual notice.

## 3. Indonesian primary-source legal map

### 3.1 Personal data — UU 27/2022 and PP 33/2026

**UU No. 27 Tahun 2022 tentang Pelindungan Data Pribadi** is in force. Official BPK PDF: https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/224884/UU%20Nomor%2027%20Tahun%202022.pdf

Relevant provisions inspected:
- Pasal 4: personal data categories, including health data as specific personal data;
- Pasal 5–15: data-subject rights;
- Pasal 16(2): processing principles, including purpose, accuracy, security and retention-related duties;
- Pasal 20–21: lawful basis and consent information;
- Pasal 25: child personal data;
- Pasal 35 and 39: security/confidentiality obligations;
- Pasal 42–45: ending processing, deletion/destruction and retention-related rules;
- Pasal 46: written notification of a personal-data protection failure within 3 x 24 hours where the statutory trigger is met;
- Pasal 51–54: controller/processor and data-protection-officer framework;
- Pasal 56: transfer of personal data outside Indonesia;
- Pasal 57 and 67–73: administrative and criminal sanctions.

**PP No. 33 Tahun 2026 tentang Peraturan Pelaksanaan UU 27/2022** was found as a 120-page PDF hosted by a local-government PPID:
http://ppid.purbalinggakab.go.id/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/peraturan_pemerintah_no._33_tahun_2026_100926115902.pdf

The copy states:
- promulgation: 16 July 2026;
- State Gazette: LN 2026 No. 88;
- Pasal 225: commencement six months after promulgation, calculated as **16 January 2027**;
- Pasal 223: transitional processing pending related institutional regulations, insofar as it does not conflict with PP 33/2026.

The document's provisions relevant to IWAG include:
- Pasal 30 and 32–37: lawful bases, consent and information;
- Pasal 38: child-data processing, parental/guardian consent and verification;
- Pasal 70–113: data-subject rights in more detail;
- Pasal 114–119: data-protection failure notification and related procedures;
- Pasal 120–122: DPIA for high-risk processing;
- Pasal 125–147: controller/processor duties, security, records, PPDP/DPO appointment criteria and responsibilities;
- Pasal 160–174: international transfer conditions;
- Pasal 184–187: administrative sanctions;
- Pasal 200–222: dispute handling.

**Verification caveat:** targeted search did not locate a central BPK/Setneg/peraturan.go.id detail record for PP 33/2026. The inspected copy is hosted by a government PPID and bears the text and LN 2026 No. 88, but the central official promulgation record and the reported TLN number still need confirmation. Do not mark TLN No. 7190 as verified. PP 33/2026 is promulgated but not yet effective on 25 November 2026; prepare for 16 January 2027 rather than describing it as already in force.

**IWAG controls:** identify the controller and processors; document each purpose and lawful basis; separate required from optional data; treat health conditions as specific personal data; implement child/guardian flows; verify DPIA/DPO triggers rather than assume either is mandatory; map Google/payment/hotel/media vendors and transfers; define breach notification and retention/deletion schedules.

### 3.2 Child protection in electronic systems — PP 17/2025

**PP No. 17 Tahun 2025 tentang Tata Kelola Penyelenggaraan Sistem Elektronik dalam Pelindungan Anak** official BPK PDF: https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/377994/PP%20Nomor%2017%20Tahun%202025.pdf

The inspected text states:
- promulgated and effective: 27 March 2025 (Pasal 50);
- Pasal 49 gives covered electronic system providers and related parties up to two years from promulgation to adjust, making the transition date 27 March 2027;
- relevant provisions include Pasal 3 (scope), Pasal 5–10 (risk/governance and child protection), Pasal 20–21 (age classification and parental consent mechanisms), and Pasal 23–29 (complaints and related safeguards).

**Applicability:** this is not automatically a rule for every physical event activity. Determine whether the IWAG platform, community platform, registration flow or any online product/service/feature falls within the PP's scope and which entity is the PSE. If covered, the 2027 transition deadline is close to the upcoming event cycle.

### 3.3 Consumer protection and online transactions

**UU No. 8 Tahun 1999 tentang Perlindungan Konsumen** official BPK record: https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/45288/uu-no-8-tahun-1999

Priority provisions:
- Pasal 4: consumer rights;
- Pasal 7–8: business-actor obligations and prohibited conduct;
- Pasal 18: prohibited standard clauses, including certain clauses that transfer responsibility or limit consumer remedies;
- Pasal 19: compensation;
- Pasal 45: dispute-resolution routes.

The current “all sales final” and broad liability waiver need a clause-by-clause review against the exact statutory text and facts. This audit does not conclude that every no-refund clause is automatically void; it does conclude that the clause cannot be treated as overriding mandatory statutory rights.

**PP No. 80 Tahun 2019 tentang Perdagangan Melalui Sistem Elektronik** should be screened where passes, merchandise or other services are offered and contracted online. Official text: https://peraturan.go.id/files3/pp-no-80-tahun-2019_asli.pdf

**IWAG controls:** identify the actual seller/contracting party and any platform/agent; show total prices, pass inclusions, restrictions, transfer rules and material changes; define refunds for each cancellation scenario; provide accessible complaint and statutory dispute routes; align the T&C, registration FAQ, payment-provider terms and hotel terms.

### 3.4 Business licensing, OSS and KBLI

**PP No. 28 Tahun 2025 tentang Penyelenggaraan Perizinan Berusaha Berbasis Risiko** is recorded by BPK as in force from 5 June 2025, LN 2025 No. 98, TLN No. 7115. It repealed PP No. 5 Tahun 2021. Official record: https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/319773/pp-no-28-tahun-2025

Do not use PP 5/2021 as the current general OSS framework without checking the transition and current OSS record.

Official OSS KBLI conversion pages inspected:
- former 82301 (MICE) converts to KBLI 2025 code 82300: https://oss.go.id/en/kbli/konversi/82301
- former 82302 has a conversion result listing 90391 and 93191: https://oss.go.id/en/kbli/konversi/82302

**Open issue:** IWAG's mixed convention includes workshops, talks, games, exhibitions, Dealers Den, panel activities and possibly performances. Do not select a single KBLI or risk level from a keyword. Obtain the organiser's actual NIB and ask OSS/competent licensing authority which KBLI 2025 activities cover the actual bundle of services. The former 82302 code's split means the old code cannot simply be copied forward.

### 3.5 Public event permits and police coordination

**Perpol No. 7 Tahun 2023 tentang Teknis Perizinan, Pengawasan dan Tindakan Kepolisian pada Kegiatan Keramaian Umum dan Kegiatan Masyarakat Lainnya** official BPK PDF: https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/388498/Perpol%20Nomor%207%20Tahun%202023.pdf

Relevant provisions inspected:
- Pasal 4(1): public crowd activities include festivals, bazaars, exhibitions, sports activities, filming, activities involving foreigners and certain public meetings; Pasal 4(2) includes public performances/concerts and contests.
- Pasal 5: identifies criteria for public crowd events requiring security, including public-order risk, large crowds involving people/groups/organisations, proximity to vital objects and relevant commercial activities. The precise trigger must be assessed with the competent police authority; do not assume every indoor hotel convention automatically follows the same permit path.
- Pasal 8(1): international-scale activities include activities organised by foreign organisations/legal entities/foreign persons or activities involving foreign persons.
- Pasal 9(3): competent police authority by scale includes Baintelkam Polri for national/international activities, Polda for provincial, Polres for city/regency, and Polsek for subdistrict.
- Pasal 10: administrative documents include organiser committee list, venue responsible party's approval, relevant recommendation, organiser statement, organisation's AD/ART if applicable, foreign participants' passport/visa if involved, and power of attorney if filed by proxy.
- Pasal 12(7): permit application deadlines are at least 14 working days (regional), 21 working days (national), and 30 working days (international) before the event.
- Pasal 15–16: document verification and coordination, including meetings, site surveys and relevant recommendations.
- Pasal 18: permit issuance time after a complete application is up to 4/7/14 working days according to scale.
- Pasal 23–29: supervision before/during/after and police actions for non-compliance.

Perpol 7/2023 cites **PP No. 60 Tahun 2017** as its underlying instrument. The Directorate General of Legislation records PP 60/2017 as **Berlaku**, promulgated 29 December 2017, LN 2017 No. 311, TLN No. 6174: https://peraturan.go.id/id/pp-no-60-tahun-2017. The parent instrument's official status and metadata are therefore verified. For an actual permit decision, read the operative PP text together with Perpol 7/2023 and confirm the competent authority's current requirements.

**Critical facts to confirm:** identify whether the event is organised by a foreign organisation, legal entity or person; whether foreign persons participate in a formal capacity (for example, as speakers, performers, workers/contractors, exhibitors/dealers or formal delegates); and how ordinary foreign attendees are treated. Perpol 7/2023 refers to activities involving foreigners and includes foreign-person documentation requirements, but do not assume that one ordinary foreign attendee alone automatically makes an event international-scale. Confirm the classification and competent police authority against the actual facts, including Article 8(1), before relying on a permit pathway.

## 4. Scholarly evidence — operational guidance, not law

The following research is supplementary evidence. It does not create legal obligations or replace Indonesian venue/fire/police/health requirements.

- Koski, Kouvonen & Sumanen (2020), *Preparedness for Mass Gatherings: Factors to Consider According to the Rescue Authorities*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17041361. A qualitative study of 15 Finnish rescue-authority specialists highlights early multi-agency planning, emergency-plan review, organiser competence, access routes, fire safety, weather/environmental factors, first aid and communication. Its Finnish setting and qualitative design limit legal transfer to Indonesia.
- Wiyono et al. (2023), *Improving public safety in events of mass gathering: The 2022 Kanjuruhan Stadium Disaster in Indonesia*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/puh2.139. This is a review/commentary on a stadium disaster, not a convention standard. Its transferable governance lessons are verified capacity, usable exits, clear command roles, emergency communication and medical readiness; stadium-specific ratios or procedures should not be copied into a convention.
- Jiménez-Andres (2024), *Accessibility of large events: an empirical study of the Expo 2020 Dubai*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10209-023-01079-7. A case study identifies gaps between advertised and usable accessibility, including navigation, facilities, information discoverability and staff knowledge. Its sample and Expo setting limit generalisation.
- Herbert, Smith & MacLennan (2025), *“It’s Like All of My Senses and My Body Become More Awakened”: Autistic Adults’ Experiences of Attending Live Music*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/25739581251375879. Qualitative work suggests advance information, quiet/reset spaces and informed staff can support attendance, while sensory preferences vary between individuals.
- van Remoortel et al. (2020), *Prediction modelling studies for medical usage rates in mass gatherings: A systematic review*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0234977. The review rates predictive-model evidence as low certainty and warns against universal staffing ratios; event-specific and locally validated data are preferable.
- Fileborn, Wadds & Tomsen (2020), *Sexual harassment and violence at Australian music festivals: Reporting practices and experiences of festival attendees*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0004865820903777. Relevant to reporting-system design, but Australian festival evidence is not Indonesian law.
- Button et al. (2024), *Preventing Sexual Harm in Nightlife Settings: A Scoping Review*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-024-02872-y. Supplementary evidence for prevention and response design; nightlife settings are not identical to conventions.

## 5. V2 policy and operational changes indicated by this audit

1. **Terms & Conditions:** identify the actual contracting party; define pass seller/platform roles; add total-price and material-change notices; rewrite refund rules by scenario; preserve mandatory consumer rights; clarify hotel booking roles; add complaint/dispute escalation without excluding statutory remedies.
2. **Privacy Policy:** identify the controller; list processors/recipients by role; document lawful basis per purpose; disclose health data as specific personal data; add child/guardian processing; state retention by category and lawful exceptions; explain international transfers; provide breach handling; explain public photo/video uploads and takedown.
3. **Code of Conduct:** add accessibility/accommodation; child-safeguarding and guardian handover; emergency and medical procedures; clear reporting channels; evidence/confidentiality/anti-retaliation; objective restricted-item/dress rules; proportionate sanctions and review.
